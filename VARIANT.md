# Variant B — To: Mum (job lost, never told)

## Concept
A confession to Mum about money/work: a job lost in March, never told.
Every Sunday she calls and asks how work is; the sender says "fine". The
message itself contains the act of typing and deleting it — the reason it
stayed in drafts (shame + not wanting to hear her voice go quiet with
worry) is felt inside the words, never explained after them. The To: line
is load-bearing: "your call", "your voice" only make sense addressed to
Mum, and the Sunday-call ritual is a mother/child ritual — swapping the
addressee would break the message. First person, plain language, one
specific detail (March). No joke, no greeting, no motivational line, no
explanation after the message.

## Versions (To: Mum = 8 chars incl. newline; total = 8 + message chars)

- **v1** — msg 142, total 150
  > I lost my job in March and I still haven't told you. Every Sunday you ask how work is going and I say fine, because I don't want you to worry.
  > Weakness: states the reason ("because I don't want you to worry") instead of letting it be felt; flat, no unsent act in the message.
- **v2** — msg 118, total 126
  > I lost my job in March. Every Sunday you ask how work is, and I say fine, because I'd rather lie than watch you worry.
  > Sharper ("I'd rather lie"), but "watch you worry" is generic and the reason is still stated as an explanation.
- **v3** — msg 137, total 145 (money angle)
  > I borrowed $400 from your savings for rent and told you my pay was late. I've been putting it back $20 at a time and you haven't noticed.
  > Specific ($400, $20) and Mum-necessary (her savings), but no felt reason it stayed unsent — "you haven't noticed" reads smug, almost boastful, not afraid.
- **v4** — msg 136, total 144 (money angle)
  > I took $400 from your savings for rent and said my pay was late. I'm putting it back $20 a week, and I pray you never check the balance.
  > Better: "I pray you never check the balance" carries fear. But "pray" is slightly performative, and the theft framing risks reading as a joke setup rather than a confession.
- **v5** — msg 127, total 135
  > I lost my job in March. When you call on Sundays and ask how work is, I say fine, because your voice goes quiet when you worry.
  > "Your voice goes quiet" is the most vivid, Mum-specific image so far — but the unsent act itself is only implied; nothing in the text says it was typed and never sent.
- **v6** — msg 136, total 144 ⭐ FINAL
  > I lost my job in March. Every Sunday after your call I type this and delete it. I'd rather lie about work than hear your voice go quiet.
  > The unsent act is IN the message ("I type this and delete it"), the felt reason is in the words ("I'd rather lie… than hear your voice go quiet"), and the To: line is essential (the Sunday call, her voice). Nothing is explained after the message.
- **v7** — msg 157, total 165
  > I lost my job in March. I type this every Sunday after your call, then delete it, because I'd rather lie about work than hear your voice go quiet with worry.
  > "Because" + "with worry" turns the felt reason into a stated explanation — exactly what the judge brief warns against. Longer, weaker.
- **v8** — msg 149, total 157
  > I lost my job in March. Every Sunday after your call I type this, then delete it. I'd rather lie about work than hear your voice go quiet with worry.
  > Same flaw as v7 ("with worry" states the reason); the comma break also softens the type/delete beat.

## Why v6 wins
Against the judge brief, in order: (1) clean two-part confession, To: + one-line message; (2) the reason it stayed in drafts is felt — typing and deleting every Sunday, preferring a lie to her voice going quiet — not stated; (3) To: Mum and the message need each other (the Sunday call, "your voice", only fit Mum); (4) it reads as typed and never sent, not written to be sent — no joke, greeting or uplift; (5) one line, no explanation after it. The money variants (v3/v4) are specific but lack the felt unsent act; v1/v2/v7/v8 state the reason instead of showing it. v6 is the only version that hits all five criteria at once.

## Final text (144 chars total, message 136 chars)
```
To: Mum
I lost my job in March. Every Sunday after your call I type this and delete it. I'd rather lie about work than hear your voice go quiet.
```

## Honest self-assessment vs the judge brief
- Strengths: specific (March, Sunday call, typing/deleting), honest, first-person, plain language; the unsent act and the felt reason are both inside the message; To: and message are inseparable.
- Risks: the field is 110+ entries and only the top 2 pay; other entries may carry a sharper single image (a named amount owed, a deathbed timing). The "I'd rather lie" framing could read as defiance to a strict judge, though the second half ("than hear your voice go quiet") softens it into protection, not pride. Total 144 chars is well under the 260 cap — the text is complete, not padded; padding it would dilute it.
- Overall: the strongest version of this concept; it matches the proven winning pattern (one concrete scene + one turn, iterated v5–v8 on a single concept) as closely as this concept allows.
